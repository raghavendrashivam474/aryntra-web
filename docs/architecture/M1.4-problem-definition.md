## Selected Problem Domain

**Engineering architecture decisions under constraint.**

Specifically: a small engineering team must choose between two or more
technical approaches. The choice has consequences across time, cost,
team capability, and risk. The team currently makes this decision
through discussion, instinct, and incomplete comparison.

This is not a hypothetical domain. It is the most common high-stakes
decision in software organisations. It happens weekly. It is rarely
documented. Its consequences are rarely traced back to the original
decision logic.

---

## Why This Domain

It was selected for four reasons:

1. **Concrete inputs exist.** Architecture decisions have real,
   nameable inputs: team size, budget, deadline, performance
   requirement, existing stack, risk tolerance.

2. **Alternatives are enumerable.** The decision space is bounded.
   There are two to five realistic options, not infinite.

3. **Trade-offs are real.** No option dominates. Every choice
   sacrifices something.

4. **The problem is unsolved by existing tools.** Current tools
   (Jira, Confluence, ADRs) record decisions after the fact.
   None actively support the reasoning process before the decision.

## Problem Statement
Problem:
Engineering teams make architecture decisions under time pressure,
incomplete information, and competing constraints — with no
structured support for reasoning through trade-offs.

Who experiences it:
Small to mid-size engineering teams (2–20 engineers).
Tech leads and engineering managers.
Founders making early technical choices.

What decision are they making:
Which technical approach, framework, architecture pattern,
or infrastructure strategy to adopt for a given requirement.

What information do they currently use:
Personal experience and preference.
Peer opinion.
Blog posts and benchmark articles of unknown applicability.
Prior decisions made in different contexts.
Gut feel about risk.

What makes the decision difficult:
Constraints interact. A budget constraint may rule out an option
that would otherwise satisfy the performance requirement.
Constraints are not always explicit — they are discovered mid-decision.
Trade-offs are asymmetric — teams often overweight short-term speed
and underweight long-term maintainability.
The decision-maker is often also emotionally attached to one option.

What alternatives exist:
Always at least two technical options.
Sometimes a "do nothing / defer" option.
Sometimes a hybrid approach.

What constraints exist:
Budget (monthly infrastructure cost)
Team capability (does the team know this technology?)
Delivery deadline
Performance requirement (latency, throughput, scale)
Operational complexity tolerance
Vendor lock-in tolerance

What can go wrong:
The chosen option fails to meet a constraint that was not
surfaced during the decision.
The trade-off accepted was not consciously understood.
The decision cannot be reversed once dependencies accumulate.
The reasoning is never recorded — the same decision is made
again six months later under different people.

What would a better decision process look like:
The team inputs the decision context explicitly.
The system surfaces the constraints and asks about any that
are missing.
The system evaluates each alternative against each constraint.
The system identifies which trade-offs the team is implicitly
accepting.
The system produces a recommendation with a clear explanation
of the reasoning.
The team retains the right to override — but now they override
with awareness.
The decision is recorded with its reasoning attached.

## What This Problem Is NOT

This is not:

- A general AI assistant that answers any technical question.
- A search engine for documentation.
- A code generator.
- A chatbot.

It is a structured reasoning tool for a specific class of decision.

---

## Scope Boundary for M1.4

The prototype will handle exactly one decision type:

> **Architecture choice: Option A vs Option B (vs Option C)**
> **Under: budget, team capability, deadline, performance constraints**

This is the minimum surface area capable of testing the hypothesis.
